abstract: Plants have expanded various biosynthetic enzyme families to produce a
  wide diversity of natural products; however, most enzymes encoded in plant 
  genomes remain uncharacterized, highlighting the need for new functional 
  genomic approaches. Here, we report a platform enabling the rapid functional 
  characterization of plant family 1 glycosyltransferases, which serve important
  roles in plant development, defense, and communication. Using 
  substrate-multiplexed reactions, mass spectrometry, and automated analysis, we
  screen 85 enzymes against a diverse library of 453 natural products, for a 
  total of nearly 40,000 possible reactions. The resulting dataset reveals a 
  widespread promiscuity and a strong preference for planar, hydroxylated 
  aromatic substrates among family 1 glycosyltransferases. We also characterize 
  glycosyltransferases with an unusually wide substrate scope and with a 
  non-canonical Cys-Asp catalytic dyad. This work establishes a 
  widely-applicable enzymatic screening pipeline, reflects the immense 
  glycosylation capability of plants, and has implications in biocatalysis, 
  metabolic engineering, and gene discovery.
